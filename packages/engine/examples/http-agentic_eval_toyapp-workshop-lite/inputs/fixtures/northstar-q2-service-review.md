# Northstar Services Q2 2026 Service Review

This is a synthetic management report for testing the Northstar report-analysis assistant. It covers Singapore operations from 1 April through 30 June 2026. Unless a section says otherwise, comparisons are with Q1 2026. Monetary figures are in Singapore dollars. The report records observations and data gaps; it does not establish that one operational change caused another.

## Executive overview

Revenue was $4.2 million in Q2, up 18% from Q1. The increase was reported alongside a higher service-ticket volume and a shorter median time to first response. Customer complaints fell by 9% relative to Q1, but this report does not provide the absolute complaint counts. One supplier accounted for 62% of Q2 purchases, creating a concentration risk that the improved service figures do not resolve. The revenue figures are unaudited management estimates. This report covers Singapore operations only, so its results should not be presented as organisation-wide performance.

The most useful high-level reading is mixed: several service indicators improved while important financial, supplier, and measurement questions remain open. The report does not contain operating costs, profit margin, or a causal study. A reader can describe the observed changes, but cannot infer that the changes increased profitability or resulted from a specific initiative. The sections below distinguish measured values, management estimates, and information that was not collected.

## Scope and method

The service measures combine records from the Workplace Helpdesk and Claims Support teams. A ticket is counted once when it is created, even if it is later transferred between teams. First-response time is measured from ticket creation to the first human reply. The median, rather than the mean, is reported to reduce the effect of a few unusually slow cases. The two-day resolution measure counts tickets closed within 48 hours of creation. Q2 contains 91 calendar days; this report does not adjust the Q1 comparison for differences in calendar days or staffing levels.

The quarterly dataset was assembled from an internal ticket export and a management revenue worksheet. Team leads reviewed category labels, but no independent audit of the source systems was performed. The report does not provide record-level data, uncertainty intervals, or a reconciliation between ticket counts and revenue. The figures should therefore be treated as descriptive management observations. They are suitable for identifying follow-up questions, not for making a causal or statistically significant claim about performance.

## Revenue and financial interpretation

The management worksheet records $4.2 million of Q2 revenue and an 18% increase from Q1. The rounded Q2 total and percentage should not be used to reconstruct an exact Q1 amount. Management attributes the increase to a mix of continuing service contracts and additional project work, but the report does not quantify the contribution of either source. No customer-level revenue breakdown is included, and the largest customer share is unknown. An analyst should therefore avoid claims about customer diversification or the sustainability of the revenue increase.

Operating costs, capital expenditure, and profit margin are absent. The report also lacks invoice collection dates, so revenue growth cannot be translated into a cash-flow improvement. The financial results are management estimates that have not been audited. The appropriate conclusion is that reported revenue rose; the report cannot show whether profit, cash generation, or unit economics improved. A follow-up review would need the cost ledger, revenue reconciliation, and contract-level detail before making those claims.

## Service demand and channels

The two teams created 1,240 new tickets during Q2, reported as 6% more than Q1. Email accounted for 55% of new tickets, the employee portal for 35%, and phone for 10%. The channel percentages sum to 100%, but the report does not provide exact ticket counts per channel. Some tickets moved between teams after creation; those transfers were not counted as new tickets. The figures describe incoming demand rather than total work completed, because reopened tickets and work in progress at quarter end are not reported separately.

The increase in incoming tickets could reflect changed demand, easier access to the service, or changes in how requests were recorded. The report provides no experiment or independent demand measure that could separate these explanations. Team leads observed that portal usage remained substantial, but no Q1 channel split is supplied, so there is no evidence here for a quarter-over-quarter shift toward the portal. A useful next analysis would compare ticket mix, staffing, and reopening rates, using the same definitions in both quarters.

## Response and resolution

Median time to first human response was 6.2 hours in Q2, compared with 7.4 hours in Q1. This is a reduction of 1.2 hours in the reported medians. The share of new tickets resolved within two days was 81% in Q2, compared with 78% in Q1, an increase of three percentage points. These observations point in a favorable direction, but the report does not show the tail of the response-time distribution, the number of urgent tickets, or how many cases remained unresolved at quarter end.

Because only the median response time is available, a reader cannot conclude that every customer experienced faster replies. Similarly, the two-day resolution percentage may conceal differences between simple and complex requests. No confidence intervals or priority-level breakdown are provided. The report also lacks a Q1-to-Q2 staffing comparison. It is therefore not possible to attribute the improved medians and resolution share to a particular staffing or workflow change using this document alone.

## Customer feedback and complaints

Customer satisfaction was 84% favorable among 420 Q2 survey responses, compared with 79% favorable in Q1. The reported difference is five percentage points. The survey was offered after ticket closure, so people whose tickets were still open at quarter end may be underrepresented. The number of customers invited to answer and the Q1 response count are not included. Without these details, the report cannot establish that the survey sample represents all customers or that the observed difference is statistically reliable.

Customer complaints fell by 9% relative to Q1. The complaint log did not provide absolute counts or a category breakdown for this report. The relative change should not be converted into an invented count, and it should not be interpreted as proof that every complaint type improved. It is also unknown whether complaint reporting behavior changed. A sensible follow-up would obtain the Q1 and Q2 counts, review category definitions, and compare complaint rates against a consistent measure of service volume.

## Supplier concentration and continuity

One supplier accounted for 62% of Q2 purchases. The report does not include the supplier's identity, the purchase categories involved, a Q1 concentration figure, or the total value of purchases. It also does not include an approved mitigation plan or a tested alternative supplier. The 62% share is enough to flag a concentration risk, but not enough to estimate the financial exposure or the probability of disruption. It should not be described as a security incident or a confirmed service failure.

Operations staff recorded a recommendation to map critical purchase categories and identify possible substitutes. That recommendation is a proposed action, not evidence that substitutes are already available. A follow-up should confirm contract terms, exit timelines, and the impact of a supplier interruption on the two service teams. Until that information is collected, any claim that supplier dependency has been resolved would exceed the evidence in this report.

## Training and operational readiness

Of 250 staff eligible for required training, 190 had completed it by the Q2 reporting cutoff, giving a 76% completion rate. The remaining 60 eligible staff had not recorded completion by that date. The training covered incident escalation and data-handling procedures, but the report does not include assessment scores, observed behavior, or a Q1 completion figure. Completion should therefore not be presented as proof that staff can apply the procedures correctly or that operational risk has fallen.

Team leads plan to follow up with staff who have not completed the modules. The report does not state a completion deadline or assign a budget for that follow-up. It also does not connect training completion to the response, resolution, or complaint measures. An evaluation of training effectiveness would require a consistent assessment and a defined outcome measure collected before and after training.

## Data quality and stated limitations

This report uses rounded aggregate values and does not include record-level extracts. Revenue is unaudited. Absolute complaint counts, operating costs, profit margin, supplier purchase value, customer-level revenue, survey invitation count, Q1 survey response count, unresolved ticket backlog, and priority-level service measures are absent. These gaps limit what can be concluded from the headline improvements. They should be reported explicitly rather than filled in with estimates that have no source in the document.

The scope is Singapore operations only. No data from other countries or business units were supplied for this review. Some metrics compare Q2 with Q1, while supplier share and training completion have no Q1 comparator. The absence of a comparator is not evidence that those measures stayed constant. No statistical test, confidence interval, randomized intervention, or causal analysis was performed. Where the report uses words such as “improved,” it refers to the observed aggregate comparison and not to a verified cause.

## Main findings and follow-up questions

The strongest supported findings are the reported 18% rise in revenue to $4.2 million, the 1.2-hour reduction in median first response, the three-percentage-point increase in two-day resolution, the five-percentage-point rise in favorable survey responses, and the 62% supplier purchase share. Each finding has an important qualification: revenue is unaudited and lacks cost context; service measures lack priority breakdowns and uncertainty estimates; the survey's representativeness is unknown; and the supplier risk lacks a quantified exposure or mitigation plan. The 9% complaint reduction is a relative change with no absolute count.

Before using this review for a broader decision, obtain the missing financial and complaint baselines, a supplier continuity assessment, and more complete service distributions. Compare channel and staffing data using consistent definitions. Review whether the 420 survey respondents differ systematically from customers who did not respond. None of these follow-up actions has been completed in this report. The document supports a cautious summary of Q2 observations and open questions, not a claim that Northstar as a whole became more profitable, safer, or more efficient because of a particular intervention.
