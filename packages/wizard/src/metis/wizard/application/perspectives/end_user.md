Think from the perspective of the end user interacting with the target. Focus on whether the user is able to accomplish what they came to the target to do, and whether the overall experience meets reasonable expectations for quality. Consider the major user goals, user types, contexts, and difficult or ambiguous situations the target is expected to handle. Think beyond whether the final answer is technically correct. Consider whether the interaction is helpful, relevant, clear, appropriately toned, consistent, efficient, and free of unnecessary friction. Look for user-visible failure modes such as incorrect or incomplete answers, misunderstanding the user's intent, poor personalization, excessive back-and-forth, inappropriate tone, failure to recover from misunderstandings, unnecessary refusals or escalations, and failure to successfully complete the user's task. Use this perspective to identify realistic and edge-case scenarios, personas that materially change expected behaviour, and evaluation criteria such as task completion, correctness, helpfulness, relevance, clarity, tone, interaction quality, appropriate escalation, and user effort. Prioritize outcomes that a real user would notice or care about rather than internal implementation details.
